A word from the Scriptures to build your faith

Written teachings from Gary Bailey — drawn from years of ministry letters to partners and friends. Read them slowly, look up the Scriptures, and let the Word strengthen your walk with God.

Put God First

Honoring the covenant in our health and our finances

Many Christians, because of improper instruction or a lack of teaching, are still shaky in their faith when it comes to divine healing. We have so much available to us today in medical care and medicine that some never even think to ask God for help. Let me say this plainly at the outset: I thank God for doctors, hospitals, and the medical care we have. I have personally benefited from a doctor’s care, and I appreciate the medical profession’s desire to heal hurting humanity. There is no sin in going to a doctor or taking medicine when you need it.

But as God’s children we should keep one thing constantly in mind: we are in covenant with God, whose very name is Healer.

If thou wilt diligently hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God… I will put none of these diseases upon thee… for I am the LORD that healeth thee.Exodus 15:26

If we are going to grow in faith concerning healing, we must put God first in every area of our lives — including the health of our bodies. It is the same principle we learn about our finances: to prosper, we must first understand that God is our source of supply. He wants us to acknowledge Him as Lord over our money, and He is pleased when we trust Him to meet our needs.

But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him.Hebrews 11:6

If our faith gives God pleasure, then we ought to consider that any time we face a physical or financial need. Abraham, the father of our faith, was careful to honor his covenant with God. After rescuing Lot and defeating the kings who had taken him, Abraham refused to let the king of Sodom take the credit: “I have lift up mine hand unto the LORD… that I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet… lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich” (Genesis 14:22–23). He gave God the glory for every blessing.

I cannot tell you how many times my wife and I have come face to face with a financial dilemma, paid our tithe or gave an offering first, and then watched God meet our need. I can say boldly: Jehovah Jireh, the Lord who supplies, has never failed us.

Where most people make their mistake is in not acknowledging God first. They try everything available to them, and only as a last resort turn to the Word of God or call for the elders of the church. But coming to God ought not to be the last resort — it should be the first thing we do.

Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him… And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up.James 5:14–15

Scripture even gives us a warning. King Asa, in the thirty-ninth year of his reign, was diseased in his feet, “yet in his disease he sought not to the LORD, but to the physicians” (2 Chronicles 16:12). It was not that he went to physicians that cost him — it was that he never put God first. How different things could have been if Asa had sought God, prayed, and set his heart right.

Put God first when it comes to your health, your finances, and everything else. Have you asked Him for what you need? Honor Him today, and ask big. You will be glad you did.

The God of All Comfort

Peace in the middle of the storm

Our God is the God of all comfort. He comforts us in our distresses so that we, in turn, can comfort others.

Blessed be God… the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble.2 Corinthians 1:3–4

Think of the word comfort, and think of the word comfortable. Our heavenly Father wants us to be comfortable in Christ — at ease, contented, calm. When the storms of life rage around us, we can still be at peace. “Let not your heart be troubled… Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you” (John 14:1, 27). The peace God gives is settled deep in the heart. There may be turmoil all around, yet at our center, in our spirit, we are at rest.

Paul described this beautiful paradox: “We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed; we are perplexed, but not in despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; cast down, but not destroyed” (2 Corinthians 4:8–9). It is cold outside, but we are warm and cozy inside. Though we are pilgrims just traveling through this life, we have a refuge, a very present help in time of trouble.

Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.1 Peter 5:7

Don’t carry a burden one more minute. Give it to Him. He cares for you tenderly and affectionately. And He has provided His comfort in more ways than we often realize.

We have comfort by His Word: “This is my comfort in my affliction: for thy word hath quickened me” (Psalm 119:50). We have comfort by the Holy Ghost, the Comforter Jesus promised would abide with us forever (John 14:16). We have comfort through prophecy — “he that prophesieth speaketh unto men to edification, and exhortation, and comfort” (1 Corinthians 14:3) — a word in due season that says, It will be all right; this too shall pass; God is with you.

We have comfort through one another in the Body of Christ. God comforted Paul “by the coming of Titus” (2 Corinthians 7:6). O, how we need each other! Never neglect fellowship. Get with other believers; get in church. At some point every one of us will need the comfort of another believer. We have comfort in His return — His very name is Consolation, and the hope of His coming steadies the soul like an anchor (Hebrews 6:19). And we have comfort by His grace: “God, even our Father… hath given us everlasting consolation and good hope through grace” (2 Thessalonians 2:16). We don’t deserve it, but He provided it anyway.

Are you sad, distressed, or lonely today? He is your Friend. He is your comfort, and He is only a prayer away. “Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted” (Matthew 5:4). You are valuable and precious. You belong to Him — you are His child.

Faith Has a Goal

Living faith is always moving toward something

Faith has a goal. Faith has an objective. If there is one thing that will help us in our walk with God, it is understanding how faith works, because our whole life in Christ revolves around it. Again and again the Word declares that the just shall live by faith (Habakkuk 2:4; Romans 1:17; Galatians 3:11; Hebrews 10:38). And we are not only to live by faith — we are to walk by it: “For we walk by faith, not by sight” (2 Corinthians 5:7).

But what does that mean in practice? Most people you meet will say they “believe” in God. So what is the difference between simply believing in God and actually living by faith? James made the distinction plain.

Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone… Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.James 2:17, 19

I, for one, do not want to be put in the same class as the devils. Yes, we believe — but there is something more. To know that Jesus is the Son of God, that He died and rose again and shed His blood for our sins, is a wonderful revelation. But is it enough just to hear? Is it enough just to know? Bible faith must act. It is hearing and acting on the Word of God that makes the difference. Genuine faith is never dormant. Bible faith lives. Bible faith walks. Bible faith works.

The first time you heard the Gospel may not have been the day you were saved — but the first time you responded to it was. Please understand: it is not what we do but what He did that saves us. Yet we must respond to His finished work. Faith is our response. I believe — yes! I believe and I act.

So faith is always moving toward a goal. When we stop moving, our faith is failing. Where there is movement, there is life. Each day I give my faith something to work on.

Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.Hebrews 11:1

Notice — faith is now. What is the tangible substance in your life today that will see your hope come to pass tomorrow? When four friends carried a paralyzed man to Jesus, the Bible says “Jesus saw their faith” (Mark 2:5). What does Jesus see in your life? Someone once asked the pointed question: if being a Christian were against the law, would there be enough evidence to convict you?

So have a goal. Is your goal heaven? Take a step toward it today and every day after. Is your goal healing? Financial provision? A ministry of your own? What are you doing today — daily, consistently — that puts your faith to work toward it? Plan your work, and work your plan. Even a small goal counts; begin now to set things in motion.

Have you fallen? Have you failed? Get up and start again — “for a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again” (Proverbs 24:16). Paul, in a horrible prison, still said, “I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus” (Philippians 3:14). Your faith is enough to get you through this day. Tomorrow you will be closer still. Live, walk, and work your faith every day — and you will see your goal come to pass.
